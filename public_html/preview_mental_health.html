Transforming Rural Villages into Caring Ecosystems

The Sanjeevani Community Mental Health Program (CMHP) stands as a flagship initiative of Gramin Adiwasi Samaj Vikas Sansthan (GASVS). Launched in 2011, it addresses the complex intersection of rural poverty, geographical isolation, deep-seated social stigma, and the lack of last-mile psychiatric care.

Active Community Participation & Inclusion

Engaging village panchayats, Self-Help Groups (SHGs), youth clubs, and traditional healers to transform communities into inclusive, caring environments where persons with intellectual and psychosocial disabilities live with dignity.

Curtailing Human Rights Violations

Confronting systemic neglect and superstition head-on—including historic grassroots advocacy and intervention to halt human rights violations of persons with mental illness at the regional ‘Hanuman temple’ complex.

Public Policy & System Convergence

Integrating community mental health models with District Mental Health Programmes (DMHP), Primary Health Centres (PHCs), and tertiary psychiatric hospitals across Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra.

Geographic Evolution

Scaling the Sanjeevani Community Mental Health Footprint

Originally seeded with the support of The HANS Foundation and SAARTHAK, Sanjeevani has systematically expanded across tribal blocks in Chhindwara, Pandhurna, and Nagpur.

Phase I · Inception

Launched flagship grassroots screening, faith-healer sensitization, and outpatient psychiatric linkages across 80 rural villages in Sausar block, Chhindwara district.

Expanded into 169 villages across Pandhurna block alongside NALAM, Home Again, and DMHP Nagpur (Project Udaan) across Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra.

Core Architecture

Three Pillars of the Sanjeevani Model

A decentralized, community-owned support framework that combines peer solidarity, public mental health literacy, and doorstep clinical follow-up.

01
Peer Solidarity Network

Federation of Caregivers

Establishing structured, block-level Caregiver Federations across Sausar and Pandhurna blocks. Families of Persons Living with Mental Illness (PLwMI) come together for mutual assistance, emotional resilience, treatment adherence monitoring, and collective advocacy for disability certificates, pensions, and social entitlements.

Mental Health Resource Centre

Programmatic Ecosystem

Four Flagship Mental Health & Inclusion Initiatives

Implemented in collaboration with national mental health institutions, philanthropic partners, and district health administrations.

Sausar & Pandhurna
Since 2011 · 282 Villages Reached

Sanjeevani Community Mental Health Programme

Our foundational rural mental health model operating across 113 villages in Sausar and 169 villages in Pandhurna. Integrates village-level screening, monthly psychiatric camps, street theatre (Nukkad Natak), and caregiver self-help federations.

  • Early identification of severe & common mental disorders
  • Psychiatric camp referrals & free psychotropic medicine access
  • Livelihood seed grants & social entitlement linkages
Partner: The Banyan
Pandhurna Block · Doorstep Care

NALAM – Community Mental Health & Well-Being

A grassroots, field-based holistic well-being programme in Pandhurna implemented through community mobilisers. Connects individuals living with untreated psychosis and severe mental illness to outpatient psychiatric care, family psychoeducation, and social welfare.

  • Intensive home visits by trained village-level NALAM workers
  • Continuous medication adherence & relapse monitoring
  • Restoring family bonds, daily grooming & social reintegration
Inclusive Supported Living
De-Institutionalisation Model

Home Again – Community Living with Dignity

Implemented in partnership with The Banyan, Home Again transitions women and individuals with long-term institutional histories out of psychiatric facilities into rented, family-like homes right inside rural communities with dedicated personal assistants.

  • Shared community homes with 24/7 compassionate caregiver support
  • Participation in neighbourhood festivals, markets & daily life
  • Choice-based routines, vocational engagement & autonomy
Public System Convergence
6,543+ Frontline Stakeholders Trained

DMHP Nagpur (Project Udaan) & System Strengthening

Working closely with public health authorities in Maharashtra and Madhya Pradesh to embed mental health care into primary health centres, train 6,543+ frontline health workers, and cover a population footprint of over 17.5 Lakh citizens.

  • Training ASHAs, ANMs, medical officers & rural practitioners
  • Strengthening District Mental Health Programme (DMHP) clinics
  • Hospital-to-community linkage with Regional Mental Hospital, Nagpur
End-to-End Care Pathway

6-Stage Community Mental Health Continuum

How GASVS bridges the 85%+ treatment gap in rural mental healthcare through a structured, human-rights-centred intervention cycle.

1. Doorstep Screening & Identification

Trained village mobilisers and ASHAs identify individuals showing symptoms of psychosis, depression, epilepsy, or intellectual disability across 282+ villages.

2,263+ Identified

2. Psychiatric Camp & OPD Linkage

Connecting identified individuals to visiting psychiatrists, block health camps, DMHP clinics, and Regional Mental Hospital Nagpur for clinical diagnosis.

Clinical Care Linkage

3. Free Medication & Adherence

Ensuring uninterrupted access to prescribed psychotropic medications alongside fortnightly doorstep visits to prevent treatment drop-out and relapse.

Zero Cost to Families

4. Psychosocial & Family Counseling

Individual supportive therapy, cognitive motor activities, and family psychoeducation to replace blame and superstition with empathy and informed care.

Holistic Well-Being

5. Anti-Stigma Street Theatre & Literacy

Nukkad Natak performances, wall paintings, SHG dialogues, and faith-healer sensitization reaching 19,328+ rural residents to dismantle myths around mental illness.

19,328+ Sensitized

6. Livelihoods, Rights & Entitlements

Facilitating UDID disability cards, Niramaya health insurance, social security pensions, and micro-enterprise support so recovering individuals regain economic dignity.
