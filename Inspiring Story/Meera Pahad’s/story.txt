From Social Exclusion to Community Leadership

Meera Pahad’s Inspiring Journey as a Pashu Sakhi

From the margins of society to becoming a trusted livestock healthcare provider, Meera’s story is a powerful testament to women’s empowerment through skills, opportunity, and community-based development.

A Journey That Began with Exclusion

In Nanda village, located in the challenging terrain of Junnardeo block, 40-year-old Meera Pahad had long experienced social exclusion and discrimination. Despite being educated, Meera and her family faced marginalisation because of their Scheduled Caste identity and were compelled to live on the outskirts of the village. Her livelihood depended on goat-rearing, an essential source of income that often received little recognition or respect. For Meera, gaining social acceptance and securing a dignified place within the community remained a distant aspiration.

Skills That Opened New Doors

A turning point came when Meera joined the Pashu Sakhi programme, an initiative that equips rural women with practical livestock healthcare skills and enables them to become local animal health service providers.

Through the programme, Meera gained knowledge and hands-on skills in identifying common livestock diseases, providing basic treatment, administering vaccinations, and promoting preventive animal healthcare practices. The training strengthened her confidence and equipped her with the expertise to support livestock-owning families in her community.

What began as a learning opportunity soon became a pathway to economic independence, social recognition, and personal transformation.

Standing Strong During a Livestock Health Crisis

In July 2024, an outbreak of Foot-and-Mouth Disease threatened livestock in Nanda village, putting the livelihoods of several families at risk. As concern spread among livestock owners, Meera stepped forward to help.

Drawing on the knowledge and skills she had acquired through the Pashu Sakhi programme, she supported affected goat owners, helped identify signs of illness, and provided appropriate basic care and guidance on disease prevention and control. Her timely response helped families access livestock healthcare support when they needed it most.

For the community, this was more than a response to a disease outbreak. It was the moment Meera’s skills became visible, her contribution was recognised, and longstanding social perceptions began to change.

From Social Marginalisation to Community Recognition

The woman who had once been overlooked was now someone her neighbours approached for advice and support. Livestock owners began recognising her knowledge, valuing her services, and trusting her expertise. Her role as a Pashu Sakhi helped her build stronger relationships within the community and establish herself as a dependable local resource for animal healthcare.

Today, Meera earns approximately ₹8,000 per month through her livestock healthcare services. Her expertise is sought not only in Nanda but also in neighbouring communities. This income has strengthened her livelihood while giving her greater confidence, independence, and a renewed sense of purpose.

For Meera, the transformation goes beyond financial gains. It represents the recovery of dignity, the opportunity to participate more fully in community life, and the recognition she deserves as a skilled professional and a woman capable of leading change.

The Impact of Empowering Rural Women

Meera’s journey demonstrates how skill development linked to local livelihood opportunities can create lasting change. By investing in rural women’s capacities and recognising their potential, community-based programmes can strengthen essential services while creating pathways to economic independence and social inclusion.

Her story also highlights the importance of accessible livestock healthcare in protecting rural livelihoods, particularly for families that depend on animal husbandry for their income and security.

Meera is more than a Pashu Sakhi. She is a symbol of resilience, self-reliance, and social transformation. Her journey reflects GASVS’s commitment to empowering rural women, strengthening community-based livelihoods, and creating opportunities for individuals to overcome exclusion and realise their potential.

When women gain the skills and opportunities to lead, they transform not only their own lives but also the communities around them.

A skilled woman. A stronger livelihood. A more inclusive community.
